---

2. Manifold Definition

A multimodal manifold is a patch‑structured soft manifold:

\[
\mathcal{M} = \bigcup{i=1}^{N} \mathcal{M}i
\]

where each patch:

- corresponds to a modality or sub‑modality  
- has its own curvature field  
- has its own spectral signature  
- has soft boundaries connecting it to adjacent patches  

Typical patch types:

- Text Patch \( \mathcal{M}_{\text{text}} \)  
- Image Patch \( \mathcal{M}_{\text{img}} \)  
- Audio Patch \( \mathcal{M}_{\text{aud}} \)  
- Symbolic Patch \( \mathcal{M}_{\text{sym}} \)  
- World Patch \( \mathcal{M}_{\text{world}} \)  
- Operator Patch \( \mathcal{M}_{\text{op}} \) (Phase 2)  

Each patch embeds into a shared latent space:

\[
\mathcal{M}_i \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^D
\]

with \( D \approx 80 \) in your system.

---

3. Soft Boundaries

Patches connect through soft boundaries:

\[
\partial \mathcal{M}i \sim \partial \mathcal{M}j
\]

Soft boundaries allow:

- smooth modality transitions  
- controlled curvature changes  
- bounded spectral drift  
- stable routing  
- operator‑safe traversal  

Soft boundaries are implemented as:

- differentiable transition membranes  
- curvature‑aware blending layers  
- spectral alignment constraints  

These boundaries are the geometric equivalent of “multimodal fusion layers.”

---

4. Curvature Fields

Each patch has a curvature field:

\[
\kappai(x) = \text{curvature at point } x \in \mathcal{M}i
\]

Curvature determines:

- routing behavior  
- collapse risk  
- spectral stability  
- operator sensitivity  
- holonomy accumulation  

High curvature → unstable routing  
Low curvature → stable traversal  
Negative curvature → collapse surfaces (Phase 2 operators will act here)

Curvature fields are computed from:

\[
\nabla^2 \mathbf{z}(x)
\]

and stored as part of the manifold metadata.

---

5. Spectral Fields

Each patch has a spectral signature:

\[
\sigmai = \{\lambda1, \lambda_2, \ldots\}
\]

Spectral fields determine:

- modality coherence  
- fusion stability  
- collapse detection  
- operator invariants  
- world‑state consistency  

Spectral alignment across patches is required for:

- multimodal fusion  
- routing ecology  
- Fantasy Engine world traversal  
- operator algebra invariants

6. Routing Surfaces

Routing is performed across routing surfaces:

\[
\mathcal{R}{ij} \subset \partial \mathcal{M}i \cap \partial \mathcal{M}_j
\]

Routing surfaces encode:

- transition cost  
- curvature gradient  
- spectral compatibility  
- collapse proximity  
- operator‑safe traversal paths  

Routing ecology (T‑007) will define:

- routing operators  
- routing heuristics  
- routing stability conditions  
- routing collapse detection  

---

7. Collapse Surfaces

Collapse surfaces are defined by:

\[
\det(\nabla^2 \mathbf{z}) < 0
\]

Collapse surfaces indicate:

- instability  
- modality drift  
- spectral noise  
- routing failure  
- operator hazard zones  

Collapse surfaces are used by:

- collapse operators (T‑022)  
- Fantasy Engine world events  
- multimodal safety mechanisms  

---

8. Holonomy Loops

Holonomy measures orientation change after traversing a loop:

\[
\Delta v = \oint_{\gamma} \mathcal{H}(v)\, d\gamma
\]

Holonomy loops exist:

- within patches  
- across boundaries  
- around collapse surfaces  
- inside world manifolds  

Holonomy operators (T‑024) will use this structure.
